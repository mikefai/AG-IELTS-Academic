## 📖 PART 2: ACADEMIC READING PASSAGE

### Passage: The Cognitive Evolution of Tool Making

```
[Paragraph A]
The capacity to conceptualize, fabricate, and manipulate complex tools has long been regarded 
as the definitive evolutionary milestone separating hominins from the broader primate lineage. 
For decades, physical anthropologists categorized the Oldowan stone tool industry—dating to 
approximately 2.6 million years ago in East Africa—as the earliest manifestation of deliberate 
lithic reduction. These primitive choppers and flakes, created by striking volcanic cobbles 
with a hammerstone, demonstrated that early Homo habilis possessed both the biomechanical 
hand-eye coordination and the spatial foresight to recognize fracture angles in stone.

[Paragraph B]
However, the 2015 discovery of the Lomekwi 3 archaeological site in Kenya dramatically pushed 
back the chronological horizon of technology to 3.3 million years ago, pre-dating the genus 
Homo by half a million years. Archaeologists excavated stone anvils and flakes showing clear 
evidence of knapping that likely belonged to Kenyanthropus platyops or Australopithecus 
afarensis. Neuroarchaeologists utilizing functional MRI scans of contemporary flintknappers 
have demonstrated that fabricating even these early tools recruits the same neural circuits in 
the left inferior frontal gyrus that regulate modern syntactical language processing. This 
finding suggests that the neurological scaffolding for human speech co-evolved alongside 
manual craft traditions.

[Paragraph C]
By 1.7 million years ago, the emergence of the Acheulean tool tradition signaled a profound 
cognitive leap. Unlike the opportunistically struck Oldowan flakes, Acheulean handaxes 
demanded a pre-existing mental template. Knappers had to enforce three-dimensional bilateral 
symmetry across a core, making dozens of calculated trimming strikes to achieve a teardrop 
silhouette. Cognitive scientists argue that imposing an abstract geometrical form onto raw 
stone reflects advanced executive function, working memory, and long-term hierarchical planning.
```

## 🔑 COMPLETE EXAM ANSWER KEY & MODEL RESPONSES

3. **trophosome** *(Specialized symbiotic internal organ)*
4. **DNA / genetic** *(Biotechnological diagnostic amplification)*

### Reading Answers (Questions 6–10)
6. **TRUE** *(Oldowan was recognized for decades prior to the 2015 Lomekwi discovery).*
7. **FALSE** *(Text states Lomekwi pre-dates Homo and belonged to Kenyanthropus or Australopithecus).*
8. **TRUE** *(fMRI scans show toolmaking recruits same circuits in left inferior frontal gyrus regulating syntactical language).*
9. **FALSE** *(Acheulean demanded advanced executive function and mental templates, requiring more working memory, not less).*
10. **NOT GIVEN** *(The text mentions neural co-evolution of language scaffolding, but does not state whether Australopithecus spoke a verbal vocabulary).*

### Writing Task 1: Band 8.0 Model Response
```
[Paragraph 1: Intro]
The bar chart delineates the gender distribution among university graduates in Science, 
Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM) subjects across four countries in 2022.

[Paragraph 2: Overview]
Overall, male graduates outnumbered their female counterparts in STEM fields across all four 
nations surveyed. The gender disparity was most pronounced in Germany and the UK, whereas 
Sweden exhibited the most balanced gender representation.

[Paragraph 3: Detail 1 — Male-Dominated Nations]
In Germany, male STEM graduates accounted for the highest proportion at 72%, outnumbering 
females (28%) by a ratio of more than two to one. A comparable trend occurred in the United 
Kingdom, where 68% of STEM degrees were awarded to men, compared to merely 32% to women.

[Paragraph 4: Detail 2 — More Balanced Nations]
In contrast, gender representation was considerably more equitable in Northern Europe. In 
Sweden, female STEM graduates constituted 45% of the total cohort, trailing men by just 10 
percentage points (55%). In Poland, women represented 41% of graduates, reflecting a 
substantially narrower attainment gap than in Western Europe.
```

### Writing Task 2: Band 8.5 Model Response
```
[Paragraph 1: Intro]
The transition from traditional print media to digital publications and online reading 
platforms represents one of the most profound cultural shifts of the 21st century. In my view, 
while the decline of physical print brings minor cognitive and economic disruptions, the 
democratization of information, economic affordability, and immense ecological savings 
render this digital evolution overwhelmingly advantageous.

[Paragraph 2: Body 1 — Environmental & Economic Advantages]
The primary merit of digital publishing lies in its environmental sustainability and 
unmatched accessibility. Traditional printing requires the deforestation of millions of 
trees, intensive chemical bleaching, and energy-consuming global transportation fleets. In 
contrast, digital publications eliminate paper manufacturing entirely, dramatically curbing 
carbon emissions. Furthermore, digital textbooks and news portals can be updated instantly in 
real-time, ensuring readers receive accurate, current data without the financial expense of 
purchasing newly revised editions. For students in developing nations, open-access electronic 
libraries have dismantled financial barriers to premium academic literature.

[Paragraph 3: Body 2 — Acknowledging Disadvantages & Rebuttal]
On the other hand, critics argue that digital reading induces cognitive fatigue and encourages 
superficial skimming due to constant digital notifications and screen glare. Additionally, 
the economic collapse of traditional print journalism has threatened independent regional 
newspapers. However, these challenges can be effectively addressed through specialized 
electronic-ink (E-ink) displays that replicate paper without eye strain, alongside innovative 
digital micro-subscription models that sustainably fund investigative journalism.

[Paragraph 4: Conclusion]
In conclusion, the migration from print to digital media is an overwhelmingly positive 
advancement. By eliminating paper consumption and making global knowledge instantly 
accessible to billions, digital publications foster a more informed, ecologically 
sustainable society.
```
