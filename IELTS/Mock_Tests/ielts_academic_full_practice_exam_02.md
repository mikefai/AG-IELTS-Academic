## 📖 Section 2: Academic Reading (Passages 1–3)

### Passage 1: The Bio-Mechanics of Avian Flight & Wing Morphologies
The aerodynamic principles governing avian flight have fascinated evolutionary biologists since the 19th century. Unlike fixed-wing aircraft that generate lift and thrust via separate mechanical systems (airfoils and jet turbines), birds achieve simultaneous lift and propulsion through dynamic, flapping wing strokes. High-speed stereoscopic cinematography reveals that during the downstroke, the primary flight feathers rotate to form an impermeable aerodynamic surface, creating a localized low-pressure vortex above the dorsal wing surface. Conversely, during the upstroke, the primary feathers separate like the slats of a Venetian blind, dramatically reducing aerodynamic drag.

Different ecological niches have driven remarkable diversity in wing aspect ratios. Pelagic seabirds like the wandering albatross possess extraordinarily long, narrow wings with high aspect ratios, allowing them to engage in "dynamic soaring"—extracting kinetic energy from vertical wind velocity gradients across ocean waves with minimal metabolic expenditure. In contrast, forest-dwelling accipiters exhibit short, rounded wings with low aspect ratios, enabling rapid acceleration and explosive maneuverability through dense canopy obstacles.

## ✍️ Section 3: Academic Writing Tasks (1 & 2)

### Academic Writing Task 1
**Prompt**: The bar chart below illustrates the proportion of renewable electricity generation (Hydroelectric, Wind, Solar, and Biomass) across four European countries in 2010 and 2024.

*Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant. Write at least 150 words.*

#### 🌟 Band 9.0 Model Report:
```text
The bar chart compares the percentage of electricity generated from four distinct renewable energy sources—hydroelectric, wind, solar, and biomass—in Germany, Sweden, Spain, and the United Kingdom across two reference years: 2010 and 2024.

Overall, it is immediately apparent that renewable electricity generation expanded substantially across all four nations over the 14-year period. While hydroelectric power was the predominant renewable source in 2010, wind and solar energy experienced the most dramatic exponential growth by 2024, particularly in Germany and the United Kingdom.

In 2010, Sweden led in total renewable output, with hydroelectricity accounting for 45% of its domestic electricity generation, followed by biomass at 18%, whereas solar power was practically negligible at under 1%. By 2024, Sweden maintained its lead in hydroelectricity (48%) while wind power expanded significantly to reach 28%.

Conversely, Germany and the UK demonstrated substantial shifts toward non-hydro renewables. In Germany, solar and wind generation surged from 8% and 12% in 2010 to 32% and 38% respectively in 2024, overtaking hydroelectricity (which remained static at around 5%). Similarly, the United Kingdom saw wind generation climb dramatically from 6% in 2010 to become its primary renewable contributor at 42% by 2024, with solar power rising from less than 2% to 16%.
```

### Academic Writing Task 2
**Prompt**: *Some people argue that technological automation will inevitably lead to widespread unemployment and social inequality, while others believe it will create new high-skilled industries and improve overall quality of life. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.*

*Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.*

#### 🌟 Band 9.0 Model Essay:
```text
The rapid acceleration of artificial intelligence and robotic automation has ignited contentious debate regarding the future of the global labor market. While critics contend that algorithmic workforce replacement will precipitate mass unemployment and exacerbate socioeconomic polarization, proponents argue that automation serves as a catalyst for new high-value industries and higher living standards. In my view, although transitional displacement poses acute short-term challenges, technological automation ultimately fosters long-term economic dynamism and societal prosperity provided proactive educational reforms are enacted.

On the one hand, apprehensions regarding widespread job obsolescence are not entirely unfounded. Routine cognitive and manual occupations—ranging from assembly-line manufacturing and transportation logistics to entry-level accounting and administrative data processing—are increasingly vulnerable to algorithmic displacement. When automation occurs faster than workers can retrain, structural unemployment inevitably surges, disproportionately impacting lower-skilled demographics. Furthermore, because the capital gains from automation accrue predominantly to corporate technology owners and investors rather than laborers, the economic divide between high-capital elites and displaced wage earners risks widening dramatically without progressive redistributive fiscal policies.

On the other hand, historical technological revolutions demonstrate that automation invariably generates novel occupational sectors that surpass the jobs they extinguish. The mechanization of agriculture and the emergence of the Internet initially disrupted traditional livelihoods, yet they ultimately spawned entire industries in software engineering, digital communications, and advanced service economies. Modern automation eliminates monotonous, dangerous, and repetitive tasks, allowing human labor to pivot toward creative problem-solving, emotional intelligence, and interdisciplinary research. Moreover, the enhanced productivity and cost efficiencies delivered by automated manufacturing reduce the cost of essential goods and healthcare, raising universal living standards.

In conclusion, while the threat of structural unemployment requires urgent government investment in lifelong vocational reskilling and robust social safety nets, I firmly believe that automation will fundamentally elevate the quality of human life and unlock unprecedented economic potential.
```
