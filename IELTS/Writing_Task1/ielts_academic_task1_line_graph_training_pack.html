Core Strategy: The "Split & Summarize" Method
Tactical Pedagogy

💡 The Strategy Explained

At Band 4, students try to describe each line separately across every single year, producing disjointed lists. The Split & Summarize Method organizes data into two macro groups and enforces an overview rule:

  1. Step 1 (Overview Formulation): Write "Overall,..." followed by two contrasting clauses. Group lines by general direction (Up vs. Down/Flat). Never write specific numbers in the overview.
  2. Step 2 (Body Paragraph 1 - The Upward Group): Group Perth and Brisbane together. Contrast start values (2005) with finish values (2020).
  3. Step 3 (Body Paragraph 2 - The Downward/Flat Group): Group Sydney and Melbourne. Report start, final figures, and key contrasts.
When this works: Whenever a line graph has 3–5 lines with opposing or contrasting directions.
When it does NOT work: If all lines follow identical fluctuations (in that case, group by absolute magnitude: highest lines vs lowest lines).

🔍 Two Worked Examples

❌ Band 4 Attempt (Mechanical Laundry List)

"The graph shows water. In 2005 Perth was 300. In 2010 Perth was 350. In 2015 Perth was 390. In 2020 Perth was 440. Brisbane was 220 in 2005 and 360 in 2020. Sydney was 420 then 270. Melbourne was 250."

Why it fails: No overview paragraph. No grouping. Repetitive robotic sentences. Reader gets no sense of the overall trends.

✅ Band 5.5 Target (Structured Grouping & Overview)

Overview: "Overall, household water usage in Perth and Brisbane experienced a steady upward trend over the 15-year period. In contrast, figures for Sydney declined significantly, while Melbourne remained relatively stable."

Group 1: "Looking first at the cities with increasing figures, Perth started at 300 thousand litres in 2005 and rose steadily to reach 440 thousand litres in 2020. Similarly, Brisbane saw a consistent rise from 220 thousand litres to finish at 360 thousand litres."

Why it succeeds: Clear overview without raw data; logical grouping of rising cities using cohesive connectors (Looking first at, Similarly, to reach).

[Introduction] The line graph illustrates the average daily time spent commuting by four different modes of transport in Metro City between 2010 and 2022.

[Overview] Overall, commuting times for private cars and buses increased steadily over the 12-year period. In contrast, travel time by train experienced a significant decline, while the duration of bicycle trips remained almost unchanged.

⚠️ 6 Common Band 4/5 Error Patterns & Exact Corrections

# Observed Weak Pattern Why it Fails (Examiner Perspective) Target Correction (Band 5.5)
1 "The graph shows Perth 300, 350, 390 and 440." Omits units ('000 L) and turns the description into a raw list of digits without grammatical verbs. "Perth's water usage rose steadily from 300 to 440 thousand litres."
2 "Overall, Perth was 300 in 2005 and 440 in 2020." Putting specific data into the overview destroys its function as a high-level summary. "Overall, water consumption in Perth grew significantly over the period."
3 "Sydney decreased on 150 thousand litres." Incorrect preposition 'on' instead of 'by' to indicate the margin of difference. "Sydney's usage decreased by 150 thousand litres."
4 "In 2010 it was... in 2014 it was... in 2018 it was..." Sentence starter monotony and mechanical year-by-year reporting. "Over the following eight years, the figure climbed to 58 minutes before reaching 65 minutes."
5 "Melbourne remained constant at 250 in all years." Inaccurate claim: Melbourne had minor dips and rises (240 and 260) before ending at 250. "Melbourne's consumption remained relatively stable, fluctuating around 250 thousand litres."
6 "Cars increased because traffic got worse in city." Inventing external causes not shown in the visual prompt (Task Achievement violation). Stick strictly to describing the data shown on the axes.

Core Strategy: Dynamic Trend Architecture & Synthesis
Tactical Pedagogy

💡 The Strategy Explained

Moving from Band 5 to Band 6 requires breaking out of simple "Subject + Verb + Number" templates. You must master three high-level language moves:

  1. Move 1 (Synthesizing the Macro Story): Instead of listing 4 separate trends in the overview, synthesize the fundamental transformation (e.g. "a massive shift away from fossil fuels—primarily coal—towards natural gas and renewable wind energy").
  2. Move 2 (Intersection & Overtaking Reporting): Identify the precise crossing point where a rising line overtook a falling line (e.g. "Natural gas surpassed coal just before 2000").
  3. Move 3 (Nominalization & Participle Clauses): Convert repetitive verbs into rich noun phrases and subordinate participle clauses:
    • Verb: "Coal decreased rapidly." → Noun: "Coal witnessed a precipitous drop from 65% to..."
    • Participle: "Wind energy surged to 26%, surpassing both nuclear and coal..."
When this works: On complex line graphs with intersecting paths, sharp transitions, or multi-phase shifts.
When it does NOT work: Do NOT invent reasons for why the shifts occurred (e.g., do not say "because of government green policies"). Keep interpretations strictly confined to descriptive data synthesis.

🔍 Two Worked Examples

❌ Band 5 Attempt (Fragmented & Repetitive)

"Natural gas was 5% in 1990 and it grew to 45% in 2010. But then it went down to 36% in 2020. Coal was the biggest at 65% in 1990 but it dropped very quickly to 35% in 2000 and 3% in 2020. Wind went up from 0% to 26%."

Why it stays at Band 5: Every sentence follows identical syntax. Zero nominalization. No comparative synthesis of the intersection between coal and gas.

✅ Band 6.0 Target (Synthesized & Varied Grammar)

"Commencing as the overwhelmingly dominant power source at 65% in 1990, coal generation experienced a steep and continuous contraction, plummeting to a negligible 3% by 2020. In stark contrast, natural gas saw a dramatic surge from a mere 5% to overtake coal around 1999, before peaking at 45% in 2010 and settling at 36%."

Why it achieves Band 6: Participle starter (Commencing as...); nominalization (a steep and continuous contraction); milestone and intersection reporting (overtake coal around 1999).

⚠️ 6 Common Band 5→6 Error Patterns & Exact Corrections

# Observed Weak Pattern Why it Fails (Band 5 Ceiling) Target Correction (Band 6.0)
1 "Firstly Germany was 20%, Secondly Sweden was 30%..." Mechanical numerical enumeration instead of comparative thematic grouping. "Looking first at the leading recycling nations, Sweden started at 30%, while Germany..."
2 "Germany increased because people followed green recycling laws." Unwarranted causal speculation outside the visual data. Stick strictly to describing visual trajectories and milestones.
3 "Despite of rising to 42%, France was lower than Germany." Grammatical error: "Despite of" is ungrammatical in English (confusing in spite of and despite). "Despite rising to 42%, France remained below Germany."
4 "Germany overtook Sweden in 2005, it reached 68% in 2020." Comma splice error: joining two independent clauses with only a comma. "Germany overtook Sweden around 2005, subsequently reaching 68% in 2020."
5 "Spain increased from 12% to 15% in 2000, then 18% in 2010, then 20% in 2020." Data dump: reporting every intermediate 2% step rather than noting a plateau. "Spain exhibited modest gains, rising gradually from 12% to plateau at 20%."
6 "The graph shows a big jump." (Vague colloquial phrasing) Informal and imprecise lexical resource. "The graph demonstrates a dramatic expansion in recycling capacity."

The line graph illustrates the proportion of Canadian adults participating in regular exercise across four age categories over a 20-year period from 2000 to 2020.

Overall, physical activity levels rose among all adult groups aged 30 and above, with the 50–64 demographic demonstrating the most pronounced growth. In stark contrast, the youngest age bracket (18–29) experienced a continuous decline, relinquishing its position as the most active cohort.

Looking first at the younger cohorts, adults aged 18–29 initially had the highest participation rate at 62% in 2000, but this figure dropped steadily to 45% by 2020. Conversely, the 30–49 age group saw steady expansion from 40%, overtaking the youngest demographic around 2012 to finish as the most active group at 58%.

Turning to older Canadians, participation among those aged 50–64 more than doubled, climbing sharply from 25% in 2000 to 52% in 2020. Meanwhile, despite starting at a modest 15% and growing gradually to 24%, the 65 and over group remained the least active category throughout the timeframe.

TA Complete: Synthesized overview with zero raw figures; highlights overtaking milestone (2012) and relative positions.
CC Natural: Grouped logically by younger vs older demographics; seamless connectors (Looking first at, Conversely, Turning to).
LR Precise: Uses nominalization ("most pronounced growth"), ratios ("more than doubled"), and accurate collocations ("active cohort").
GRA Varied: Accurate participle clauses ("relinquishing its position", "overtaking the youngest demographic") and concession structures ("despite starting at...").

Item 1: Overview Formulation (Macro Synthesis vs. Data Dump)
Extract A:
"Overall, in 2000 the 18–29 group was the highest at 62%, but in 2020 the 30–49 group was 58% and 65+ was 24%."
Extract B:
"Overall, regular physical activity increased across all age groups over 30, whereas the youngest bracket experienced a steady downward trend."
Correct Answer: Extract B (Band 6.0 standard).
Rationale: An Overview should synthesize macro trends and major contrasts without quoting specific numbers. Extract A is a Band 4/5 mistake that treats the overview as a raw data repository.
Item 2: Data Selection & Milestone Reporting
Extract A:
"The 30–49 cohort grew from 40% in 2000 to 44% in 2005, then 48% in 2010, 54% in 2015, and finally 58% in 2020."
Extract B:
"Starting at 40% in 2000, the 30–49 age group grew steadily, overtaking the youngest cohort around 2012 to peak at 58% in 2020."
Correct Answer: Extract B (Band 6.0 standard).
Rationale: Extract A mechanically recites every intermediate year (Band 4/5). Extract B highlights the start, the significant overtaking point (2012), and the endpoint (Band 6+).
Item 3: Trend & Preposition Precision
Extract A:
"Participation among those aged 50–64 more than doubled, climbing sharply from 25% in 2000 to 52% in 2020."
Extract B:
"Participation among 50–64 aged doubled with 25% until 52% in the 2000 to 2020."
Correct Answer: Extract A (Band 6.0 standard).
Rationale: Extract A uses accurate proportional language ("more than doubled") and precise prepositions ("from... to"). Extract B contains broken prepositions ("with 25% until 52%") and awkward phrasing.
Item 4: Lexical Variety vs. Repetition
Extract A:
"18-29 went down and 30-49 went up and 50-64 went up and 65+ went up slowly."
Extract B:
"While the youngest demographic experienced a steady contraction, older age brackets witnessed substantial gains."
Correct Answer: Extract B (Band 6.0 standard).
Rationale: Extract B deploys nominalization ("steady contraction", "substantial gains") and academic phrasing instead of repeating "went up/down".
Item 5: Cohesive Flow vs. Mechanical Enumeration
Extract A:
"Firstly, the 18-29 group dropped. Secondly, the 30-49 group rose. Thirdly, the 50-64 group rose."
Extract B:
"Looking first at the younger cohorts, the 18–29 group dropped steadily, whereas the 30–49 demographic expanded continuously."
Correct Answer: Extract B (Band 6.0 standard).
Rationale: Extract B groups data into logical categories with organic contrast linkers (whereas). Extract A relies on childish robotic list markers (Firstly, Secondly, Thirdly).
