# 📊 IELTS Academic Writing Task 1: Mixed Charts & Multiple Visuals (Band 5.0 ➔ 6.5)

## 📋 Module Overview
- **Exam Section**: Academic Writing Task 1 (Mixed Graphics / Multiple Visuals: Table + Bar Chart, Pie Chart + Line Graph, etc.)
- **Target Audience**: Students stuck at Band 5.0–5.5 needing a reliable formula to achieve Band 6.5+.
- **Recommended Time**: 20 Minutes | **Length**: 150+ Words | **Weight**: 33.3% of Writing Score.
- **The Core Problem for Band 5 Candidates**:
  - Seeing two different graphics and panicking.
  - Mashing both charts together into a chaotic paragraph without clear structure.
  - Writing an overview for only one of the two charts (capping Task Achievement at Band 5).
  - Listing every single data point like a spreadsheet.

---

## 🎯 1. The "Divide & Conquer" 4-Paragraph Blueprint

The absolute easiest and most reliable way to score Band 6.5+ on mixed visual prompts is the **Divide and Conquer Method**:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                       THE 4-PARAGRAPH MIXED CHART ARCHITECTURE                          |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PARAGRAPH 1: INTRODUCTION (~30–35 words)                                                |
| • Paraphrase BOTH graphics in ONE compound sentence using a linking coordinator.        |
| • Formula: "The [Graphic 1] shows [Topic 1], while the [Graphic 2] illustrates [Topic 2]... |
|                                                                                         |
| PARAGRAPH 2: THE DUAL OVERVIEW (~40–45 words)                                           |
| • Sentence 1: The main macro-trend of Graphic 1 (NO figures/percentages).               |
| • Sentence 2: The main macro-trend of Graphic 2 (NO figures/percentages).               |
| • Connector: "Overall, [Trend 1]. In addition, [Trend 2]..."                            |
|                                                                                         |
| PARAGRAPH 3: BODY 1 — DETAIL ANALYSIS OF GRAPHIC 1 (~45–55 words)                       |
| • Focus exclusively on Graphic 1.                                                       |
| • Group data into highs, lows, and notable comparisons with exact figures.              |
|                                                                                         |
| PARAGRAPH 4: BODY 2 — DETAIL ANALYSIS OF GRAPHIC 2 (~45–55 words)                       |
| • Transition smoothly: "Turning to the [Graphic 2]..."                                  |
| • Focus exclusively on Graphic 2 (or highlight cross-chart correlation if evident).     |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

---

## 🛠️ 2. Step-by-Step Engineering for Band 6.5

### Step 1: Paraphrasing the Dual Introduction
Never write two separate introduction paragraphs. Combine both in a single compound sentence:

- **Band 5 (Weak / Separated)**:  
  *"The first picture is a table. It is about consumer spending on clothes in 2020. The second picture is a bar chart. It shows spending in 2024."* ❌
- **Band 6.5+ (Compound Academic Paraphrase)**:  
  *"The table outlines consumer expenditure on clothing across five European countries in 2020, while the accompanying bar chart depicts projected spending changes by 2024."* ✅

### Step 2: The Dual-Overview Rule (Crucial for Band 6+)
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Examiner Rule**: If the task presents two charts, your Overview **MUST** summarize the main feature of **BOTH** charts. If you only write an overview for Chart 1, the maximum score you can receive for Task Achievement is **Band 5.0**.

- **Formula**:
  - `Sentence 1`: *"Overall, [Main trend or highest contributor of Graphic 1]."*
  - `Sentence 2`: *"Simultaneously, [Main trajectory or dominant sector of Graphic 2]."*
  - **Zero Figures Rule**: Do not include a single number, dollar amount, or percentage in Paragraph 2.

## 📝 3. Authentic Practice Task & Comparative Analysis

### Prompt
> **The table below shows the number of international visitors to five European cities in 2018 and 2023, while the pie chart illustrates the primary reasons for travel reported by these visitors in 2023.**  
> *Summarize the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.*

#### Visual Data Summary
- **Table (Visitor Numbers in Millions)**:
  - *Paris*: 2018 = 19.1m | 2023 = 21.5m
  - *Rome*: 2018 = 15.2m | 2023 = 14.8m
  - *London*: 2018 = 20.4m | 2023 = 20.2m
  - *Barcelona*: 2018 = 12.0m | 2023 = 15.6m
  - *Amsterdam*: 2018 = 8.5m | 2023 = 9.1m
- **Pie Chart (Visitor Motivations in 2023)**:
  - *Leisure & Sightseeing*: 52%
  - *Business & Conferences*: 26%
  - *Visiting Friends & Relatives (VFR)*: 14%
  - *Educational & Cultural Exchange*: 8%

---

### ❌ Band 5.0 Diagnostic Sample (What Keeps Students Below 6)

```text
The table gives information about visitors to 5 cities in 2018 and 2023. The pie chart shows 
why they go there.

Overall, Paris had 21.5 million visitors in 2023 and 52% of people travel for leisure. 

In the table, London had 20.4 million in 2018 and 20.2 million in 2023. Paris was 19.1 
million in 2018 and it increased to 21.5 million in 2023. Rome was 15.2 million and went down 
to 14.8 million. Barcelona was 12.0 million and went up to 15.6 million. Amsterdam was the 
lowest at 8.5 million and 9.1 million.

In the pie chart, leisure was 52%. Business was 26%. Visiting friends was 14% and education 
was 8%.
```

#### Why This Sample Fails (Examiner Diagnostic):
- **Task Achievement (Band 5.0)**: Overview includes raw figures (*"21.5 million"*, *"52%"*) which belongs in the body paragraphs; details are listed mechanically city-by-city without analytical groupings.
- **Coherence & Cohesion (Band 5.0)**: Repetitive sentence patterns (*"X was...", "Y was..."*); lacks transitional bridges between table and pie chart.
- **Lexical Resource (Band 5.0)**: Basic repetitive vocabulary (*"went up", "went down", "was"*, repeats *"million"* 8 times).
- **Grammatical Range (Band 5.0)**: Simple coordinate sentences with minimal subordination.

---

### ✅ Band 6.5 Model Essay (The Target Standard)

```text
The table outlines the volume of international tourist arrivals across five European cities 
in 2018 and 2023, while the pie chart illustrates the principal motivations for these visits 
in 2023.

Overall, international visitor numbers rose in the majority of the selected cities, with 
Paris overtaking London to become the most visited destination. Regarding travel motivations, 
leisure and sightseeing accounted for the majority of trips, whereas educational purposes 
represented the smallest share.

Examining the table in detail, Paris recorded the most substantial growth, increasing from 
19.1 million visitors in 2018 to a peak of 21.5 million in 2023. Barcelona also experienced 
marked expansion, rising from 12.0 million to 15.6 million. In contrast, London experienced 
a marginal decline from 20.4 million to 20.2 million, while Rome also contracted slightly 
from 15.2 million to 14.8 million. Amsterdam remained the least frequented destination, 
growing modestly from 8.5 million to 9.1 million.

Turning to travel purposes in 2023, holidaymaking and sightseeing constituted just over half 
(52%) of total visitor journeys. Business and corporate events formed the second largest 
category at approximately one-quarter (26%), followed by visiting friends and relatives at 
14%. Conversely, educational and cultural visits comprised a negligible fraction of only 8%.
```

## 🔍 4. Why This Model Scores Band 6.5+

| Criterion | Band 6.5 Feature in Model |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Task Achievement (Band 7.0)** | The overview clearly synthesizes both visuals in 2 sentences with zero numbers. Key trends (growth vs decline in Table; dominant vs minor reasons in Pie Chart) are accurately highlighted. |
| **Coherence & Cohesion (Band 6.5)** | Clear 4-paragraph structure. Smooth transitional phrase (*"Turning to travel purposes in 2023..."*) bridges the two visuals cleanly. |
| **Lexical Resource (Band 6.5)** | Strong academic synonyms: *volume of arrivals, principal motivations, overtaking, marked expansion, marginal decline, contracted slightly, holidaymaking, corporate events, negligible fraction*. |
| **Grammatical Range & Accuracy (Band 6.5)** | Good control of complex structures: participial clauses (*"increasing from 19.1m to a peak of 21.5m"*, *"growing modestly"*), contrast clauses (*"whereas...", "In contrast..."*). |

## 💡 5. High-Scoring Bridging Phrases for Mixed Charts

```
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------+
| FUNCTION                          | ACADEMIC PHRASAL TEMPLATES                                |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------+
| Combining Both Visuals in Intro   | "The [Visual 1] illustrates [Topic A], whilst the [Visual 2] details [Topic B]." |
| Dual Overview Opener              | "Overall, while [Visual 1 macro trend], [Visual 2 macro trend]." |
| Transitioning to Graphic 2        | "Turning to the accompanying [bar/pie/table]...",         |
|                                   | "With regard to the data presented in the [second visual]..." |
| Highlighting Proportions          | "constituted just over half", "represented roughly a quarter" |
| Highlighting Contrasts            | "In stark contrast to [City A], [City B] recorded..."     |
+-----------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------+
```
