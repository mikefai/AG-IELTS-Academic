# ✍️ IELTS Academic Writing Task 2: Discuss Both Views (Band 6.0 ➔ 7.0)

## 📋 Module Overview
- **Exam Section**: Academic Writing Task 2 (Discussion + Personal View Prompt)
- **Target Transition**: Band 6.0 ➔ 7.0
- **The Core Problem for Band 6 Candidates**:
  - Writing 80% of the essay on their favored view and giving only 2 sentences to the opposing view (lacks parity/balance, capping Task Response at Band 6.0).
  - Failing to express their own opinion in the introduction and leaving it solely to the conclusion, resulting in an unclear position throughout the response.
- **Pedagogical Objectives**:
  1. Maintain a **clear, consistent position throughout** (Intro ➔ Body 2 ➔ Conclusion).
  2. Provide **substantive, balanced parity** to both viewpoints before taking a definitive side.
  3. Master **concession and refutation sentence patterns** (*"While proponents argue X, I maintain Y because..."*).

### Concept Checking Questions (CCQs) for Teachers
- *Q: "If the prompt says 'Discuss both views and give your opinion', can I only discuss my own view?"*  
  **A:** **No.** You must thoroughly explore the rationale of *both* sides, dedicating roughly equal paragraph space to each.
- *Q: "When should I state my opinion?"*  
  **A:** **Immediately in the Introduction (Sentence 2)**, reinforced in Body 2, and restated in the Conclusion.

## 📚 2. In-Depth Study Notes: The Balanced Discussion Architecture

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            DISCUSS BOTH VIEWS ESSAY SKELETON                          |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PARAGRAPH 1: INTRODUCTION (~50 words)                                                 |
| • Sentence 1: Background Paraphrase (Acknowledge that views diverge on the issue).    |
| • Sentence 2: Thesis & Stance (Acknowledge View A, but declare your support for View B).|
|                                                                                       |
| PARAGRAPH 2: BODY 1 — EXAMINE VIEW A (The Opposing View) (~95–105 words)               |
| • Topic Sentence: "On the one hand, proponents of [View A] contend that..."           |
| • Explanation: Explain the underlying societal, economic, or logistical rationale.     |
| • Concrete Example: Provide an objective, non-personal example illustrating the case.  |
| • Concession / Limitation: Highlight the scope limitation of this approach.           |
|                                                                                       |
| PARAGRAPH 3: BODY 2 — EXAMINE VIEW B & YOUR VIEW (The Supported Stance) (~100–110 words)|
| • Topic Sentence: "On the other hand, I side with those who argue that [View B]..."  |
| • Explanation: Elaborate on the superior long-term merits and systemic advantages.   |
| • Concrete Example: Statistical, technological, or environmental evidence.            |
| • Synthesis: Tie back to the thesis, showing why View B outweighs View A.             |
|                                                                                       |
| PARAGRAPH 4: CONCLUSION (~40–45 words)                                                |
| • Sentence 1: Summarize the merits of both perspectives.                              |
| • Sentence 2: Restate your definitive position in fresh, elevated vocabulary.         |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 🔍 3. Guided Exercise: Annotated Model Essay Breakdown

### Prompt
> **Some people believe that international tourism brings significant economic benefits to host countries. Others argue that the environmental and cultural damage it causes far outweighs these advantages.**  
> *Discuss both views and give your own opinion. Write at least 250 words.*

```
=========================================================================================
                                   BAND 7.5 MODEL ESSAY
=========================================================================================
[Paragraph 1: Introduction]
The global expansion of international travel has generated intense debate regarding its overall 
societal impact. While some argue that foreign tourism acts as a vital catalyst for economic 
prosperity, others contend that its environmental degradation and cultural erosion outweigh 
such gains. In my view, although tourism generates substantial financial revenue, its long-term 
ecological and cultural repercussions require strict regulatory containment.

[Paragraph 2: Body 1 — The Economic Arguments (View A)]
On the one hand, proponents of tourism highlight its immense capacity to stimulate economic 
growth and generate employment. In developing economies and coastal regions, the influx of 
foreign capital directly finances national infrastructure, hotels, transport networks, and local 
businesses. Furthermore, the hospitality sector creates millions of jobs across diverse skill 
levels, from tour guides to service personnel. For instance, in countries such as the Maldives 
and Thailand, international tourism contributes over 20 percent of national Gross Domestic 
Product, providing essential livelihood opportunities that traditional agriculture cannot sustain. 
Consequently, tourism represents an indispensable fiscal lifeline for many host communities.

[Paragraph 3: Body 2 — Environmental & Cultural Costs + Author's Stance (View B)]
On the other hand, I agree with critics who maintain that unregulated tourism inflicts severe, 
often irreversible damage on natural ecosystems and cultural heritage. The mass influx of 
visitors places unsustainable pressure on delicate ecosystems, leading to carbon-heavy aviation 
emissions, coastal habitat destruction, and excessive plastic waste accumulation. Culturally, 
mass tourism frequently trivializes sacred traditions and inflates local real estate prices, 
pricing local residents out of historic city centers. A notable example is Venice, where 
overcrowding and cruise-ship pollution have degraded ancient canals and forced thousands of native 
citizens to relocate. Therefore, when unmitigated, the environmental and cultural costs 
surpass immediate financial returns.

[Paragraph 4: Conclusion]
In conclusion, while international tourism delivers undeniable financial dividends and 
employment generation, I firmly believe that its ecological disruption and socio-cultural 
displacement represent far greater threats. Governments must enforce sustainable tourism 
quotas and eco-taxes to ensure commercial prosperity does not occur at the expense of national 
heritage.
=========================================================================================
```

#### Line-by-Line Examiner Breakdown:
- **Task Response (Band 7.5)**: Equal paragraph length and depth for both View A (Maldives/Thailand economic case) and View B (Venice cultural/ecological case). The author's position is declared in the intro (*"In my view..."*), developed in Body 2 (*"I agree with critics who maintain..."*), and consolidated in the conclusion.
- **Coherence & Cohesion (Band 7.5)**: Paragraphs start with clear directional markers (*"On the one hand... On the other hand, I agree with... In conclusion"*). Internal cohesion uses consequence connectors (*"Consequently", "Therefore"*).
- **Lexical Resource (Band 7.5)**: High-register academic vocabulary: *catalyst for economic prosperity, ecological repercussions, influx of foreign capital, fiscal lifeline, irreversible damage, trivializes sacred traditions, socio-cultural displacement*.
- **Grammatical Range & Accuracy (Band 8.0)**: Complex sentence structures, including fronted adverbials, participial clauses (*"pricing local residents out"*, *"forcing native citizens to relocate"*), and conditional framing (*"when unmitigated"*).

## 🔑 5. Complete Answer Key & Step-by-Step Rationales

### Exercise 1 Answer Key
- **Outline 1:** ❌ **Fails Band 7.0 (Caps at Band 6.0 for Task Response).**  
  *Rationale:* The opinion is absent from the introduction and only introduced in the conclusion, violating the requirement for a *"clear position throughout"*. Additionally, the 3-sentence vs 12-sentence body paragraphs create severe imbalance.
- **Outline 2:** ✅ **Meets Band 7.0+ Standard.**  
  *Rationale:* Balanced development of both perspectives paired with an early, unwavering thesis stated in the introduction and sustained through Body 2 and the Conclusion.

### Exercise 2 Answer Key
1. **Sentence Upgrade:** *"While online education undeniably provides unmatched geographical flexibility, I contend that traditional classroom settings remain indispensable because they cultivate essential interpersonal and collaborative competencies."*
2. **Sentence Upgrade:** *"Although regulating digital platforms may temporarily mitigate malicious misinformation, I maintain that safeguarding free expression is of paramount importance since it underpins democratic transparency."*
