📘 2. Core Grammar & Meaning Systems

A. Module A: Past Simple vs Present Perfect

1. Meaning & Concept

  • Past Simple: The time period is finished and closed in the past.

- Example: "I played basketball with my classmates yesterday." (Yesterday is finished).

  • Present Perfect: The action is connected to the present moment in 3 primary ways:

1. Life Experience (time unspecified): "Have you ever watched an esports final live?"

2. Recent Result (action happened recently, result is now): "My phone battery has died (I can't message my friends now)."

3. Unfinished State / Time Period: "I've studied at this high school for two years." / "I've had three exams this week."

2. Deep Dive: "Been to" vs "Gone to"

  • Been to (Visited and returned): "Defne has been to the canteen." (She went there, got a snack, and is back with us now).
  • Gone to (Still at the destination): "Defne has gone to the canteen." (She is at the canteen right now; look for her there).

3. Deep Dive: "Since" vs "For"

  • Since + Specific Point: since 8:30 AM, since last September, since I started 9th grade, since 2021.
  • For + Length of Time: for 45 minutes, for two weeks, for three periods, for five years.

4. Signal Word Bank & Positions

  • Past Simple: yesterday, ... ago, last night/week/year, in 2022, when I was in middle school, during 8th grade.
  • Present Perfect: ever, never, just (mid-position), already (mid-position), yet (end-position in negative/questions), since, for, this morning/week (unfinished).

B. Module B: Past Simple vs Past Perfect (Storytelling)

1. Meaning & The "Earlier Past"

We use Past Perfect (had + V3) when narrating in the past and jumping back to an action that happened before the main past storyline.

  • Event 1 (First in time): The school bus had left (had + V3).
  • Event 2 (Second in time): I reached the stop (Past Simple).
  • Combined: "When I reached the bus stop, the bus had already left." (I missed the bus and had to walk!).

2. The 3-Tense Master Narrative Formula

Fluent storytellers combine three past tenses seamlessly:

1. Background Atmosphere (Past Continuous): "I was walking to class in the morning..."

2. Main Event (Past Simple): "when I suddenly opened my backpack..."

3. Earlier Background Cause (Past Perfect): "and realised that I had left my homework notebook on my desk at home!"

3. Spoken Contractions

  • I had $\rightarrow$ /aɪd/ (I'd)
  • She had $\rightarrow$ /ʃiːd/ (She'd)

🗣️ 4. Classroom Speaking Tasks (Teen & High School Edition)

Task 1: High School Warmer (Pair Questions & Model Answers)

1. Q: "Have you ever forgotten your homework or school uniform?"

Follow-Up: "When did it happen? What did your teacher say?"

Model Answer: "Yes, I have! Last term I forgot my math project at home. My teacher let me submit it during lunch break."

2. Q: "Have you ever played in a high-stakes gaming match or sports tournament?"

Follow-Up: "What game or sport was it? Did your team win?"

Model Answer: "Yes, I played in a school basketball final last winter. It was super intense, and we won by two points!"

3. Q: "Have you ever had your phone battery die at the worst possible moment?"

Follow-Up: "Where were you? How did you solve the problem?"

Model Answer: "Yes! Last month my phone died while I was waiting for the bus. I had to borrow a classmate's phone to call my parents."

4. Q: "Have you ever met a famous YouTuber, athlete, or celebrity?"

Follow-Up: "Who was it? What did you say to them?"

Model Answer: "Yes, I saw a famous gaming streamer at a tech expo in Istanbul last year. I took a quick selfie with him!"

5. Q: "Have you ever overslept and arrived late for a school exam?"

Follow-Up: "Why did you oversleep? What happened when you entered the class?"

Model Answer: "Yes, once in 9th grade my alarm failed. By the time I reached the classroom, the exam had already started!"

6. Q: "Have you ever performed on stage (singing, theater, presentation)?"

Follow-Up: "How did you feel before going on stage? How did it go?"

Model Answer: "Yes, I gave a presentation at the school science fair. I was really nervous at first, but everyone clapped in the end."

Task 2: 60-Second Monologue Speaking Cards (5 Teen Topics)

  • Card 1 (Unforgettable School Trip / Camp): Talk about the most exciting school excursion you have experienced.
  • Card 2 (The Homework Emergency - Past Perfect Focus): Talk about a stressful situation when something went wrong with your school project.
  • Card 3 (Gaming / Sports Milestone): Talk about an epic video game victory or tournament final you will never forget.
  • Card 4 (A New Skill Learned Recently): Talk about a hobby, digital skill (coding, video editing), or sport you have picked up recently.
  • Card 5 (An Embarrassing Moment in Class): Talk about a funny or awkward situation that happened at school.

Detailed Distractor Rationales

  • Item 5: (A) has been means she visited and is already back; (C) had gone has no secondary past reference; (D) went is a single past event without the present location implication.
  • Item 14: (B) has founded violates the rule that historical events in named past years take Past Simple; (C) had founded is redundant without a later past reference point.
  • Item 21: (A) has been would mean she is already back home, contradicting the sentence "she'll be back in 10 minutes".
  • Item 25: (A) gone implies the speaker is still in Cappadocia right now; in asking about life travel experience, English uses been to.
  • Item 29: (A) didn't finish ignores the keyword yet and the immediate present consequence; (C) hadn't finished is past narrative.
