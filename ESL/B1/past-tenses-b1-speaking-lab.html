🎯 The 3 Core Past Tense Rules (High School Summary)
Essential Cheat Sheet
1. Past Simple (Finished Time Box)
Use for completed events at a specific time in the past.
"I played Minecraft with my friends yesterday evening."
Signal words: yesterday, 2 hours ago, last Friday, in 2022.
2. Present Perfect (Connected to Now)
Use for life experiences, recent results right now, or ongoing teen habits.
"I've lost my school ID card (I can't enter the school right now)."
Signal words: ever, never, just, already, yet, since, for.
3. Past Perfect (The Earlier Past)
Use in storytelling when one past event happened BEFORE another past event.
"When I entered the classroom, the teacher had already started the pop quiz."
Signal frames: by the time, when, after, realised that.
👩‍🏫 Teacher's Guide: Lesson Plan for Teenagers (15yo High Schoolers)

Lesson Target: Engage 15-year-old intermediate (CEFR B1) learners in natural, spontaneous speaking using Past Simple, Present Perfect, and Past Perfect. Focus on relevant topics: school emergencies, hobbies, social media, gaming, and friendship.

🌟 The 3 Core High School Uses of Present Perfect

1. Life Experience (Ever in your life)
Time is not specified. We are asking about your whole life up to now.
"Have you ever attended a live esports tournament?"
"I've never played basketball before."
2. Recent Action with a Present Result
Something happened recently and affects right now.
"My phone battery has just died (I can't message my mum now)."
"The bell has rung; let's go to class!"
3. Unfinished State or Time Period
Started in the past and continues right now.
"I've studied at this high school for two years."
"I've had three exams this week (and the week is not over)."

📍 Critical Contrast: "Been to" vs "Gone to"

"She has been to the library."
Visited and returned! She went there, finished studying, and is back with us right now.
"She has gone to the library."
Still there right now! She left and has not come back yet. If you want her, go to the library!

⏳ "Since" (Starting Point) vs "For" (Duration)

SINCE + Starting Point in Time FOR + Length / Duration of Time
• since 8:30 AM
• since last September
• since I started 9th grade
• since 2021 		• for 45 minutes
• for two weeks
• for three periods
• for five years

🎯 Just / Already / Yet: Position Rules

• JUST (very recently): Between *have* and verb 3 $\rightarrow$ "I've just submitted my project on Google Classroom."
• ALREADY (earlier than expected): Between *have* and verb 3 $\rightarrow$ "I've already revised chapter 4."
• YET (not completed up to now): At the END of negative sentences and questions $\rightarrow$ "Have you done your biology homework yet?" / "I haven't packed my gym bag yet."
🇹🇷 Turkish High Schooler L1 Guide (Neden Karıştırıyoruz?)

Neden zorlanırız? Türkçe'de *"Dün maç yaptık"* ile *"Hayatımda hiç maça gitmedim"* cümlelerinde aynı -di'li geçmiş zaman ekini kullanırız.

3 Altın Kural:

  • Zaman belliyse (yesterday, last night, 2 days ago, in 2022) $\rightarrow$ Past Simple (V2).
  • Zaman belli değilse, tecrübe anlatılıyorsa (Have you ever...? / I've never...) $\rightarrow$ Present Perfect (`have/has + V3`).
  • *-den beri* derken Türkçe'de geniş zaman kullansak bile İngilizce'de Present Perfect şarttır: *"I know my desk partner since 9th grade" ❌ $\rightarrow$ "I have known my desk partner since 9th grade" ✅*.
❓ Concept Checking Questions (CCQs) for Teachers
  1. "Ali has gone to the football pitch." $\rightarrow$ Is Ali here in the room now? (No, he is at the pitch.)
  2. "Can has been to the football pitch." $\rightarrow$ Is Can back with us now? (Yes, he went and returned.)
  3. "I bought this gaming headset on Friday." $\rightarrow$ Is Friday finished? (Yes.) Can we say 'have bought'? (No, because Friday is a finished past time.)
  4. "She has played volleyball for 3 years." $\rightarrow$ Does she still play volleyball today? (Yes.)
📙 Module B: Past Simple vs Past Perfect
Storytelling & Narrative

We use the Past Perfect (had + V3) for ONE main purpose: The Earlier Past in a Story.

📖 Visual Timeline: Two Past Events (Which Happened First?)
Event 1 (First in Past)
PAST PERFECT (had left)
Event 2 (Second in Past)
PAST SIMPLE (arrived)
NOW
Present time

"When I arrived at the school gate, the school bus had already left." (Event 1: Bus left $\rightarrow$ Event 2: I arrived $\rightarrow$ Result: I had to walk to school!).

🗣️ Spoken Form Focus: Contractions "I'd" /aɪd/ & "She'd" /ʃiːd/

In natural speech, native speakers almost always contract had:

• "I had forgotten my PE kit." $\rightarrow$ "I'd forgotten my PE kit." (/aɪd fərˈɡɒt.ən/)
• "She had already finished the exam." $\rightarrow$ "She'd already finished the exam." (/ʃiːd ɔːlˈred.i ˈfɪn.ɪʃt/)

🎬 The 3-Tense Master Narrative Formula

How fluent speakers combine 3 past tenses in a single story:

1. Background Atmosphere
Past Continuous: "I was walking home from school in the rain..."
2. Main Event / Action
Past Simple: "...when I suddenly reached into my bag..."
3. Earlier Background Reason
Past Perfect: "...and realised I had left my keys in the science lab!"

🎬 5 High-Frequency Storytelling Frames for Teenagers

1. "By the time the bell rang..."
"By the time the bell rang, I had finished all 20 math problems."
2. "I realised that I had..."
"When the teacher called my name, I realised I had forgotten my assignment."
3. "When + Past Simple, + Past Perfect"
"When the match ended, our team had scored five goals!"
4. "After + Past Perfect, + Past Simple"
"After I had charged my phone, I called my friends on Discord."
💡 High School Pro Tip: Keep It Natural!
When words like "before" or "after" make the order obvious, Past Simple for both events is completely natural:
"I ate dinner after I finished my homework." (100% natural and correct in everyday speaking!).

🔍 Meaning Contrast: Did you catch the concert?

"When we got to the arena, the band started playing."
First we arrived, and then immediately the band started. We saw the entire concert!
"When we got to the arena, the band had started playing."
The band started BEFORE we arrived. We were late and missed the opening tracks!
❓ Concept Checking Questions (CCQs) for Teachers
  1. "When the teacher entered, the students had stopped talking." $\rightarrow$ When did the talking stop? (Before the teacher entered.)
  2. "By the time I found my gym shoes, the match had started." $\rightarrow$ Which happened first: finding shoes or match starting? (Match starting.)
  3. "I didn't have my phone because I had lost it yesterday." $\rightarrow$ Why was I without a phone? (Losing it happened earlier.)
