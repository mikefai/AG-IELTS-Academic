🎯 The 3 Core Past Tense Rules (B1 Summary)
Essential Guide
1. Past Simple (Finished Time)
Use for actions finished at a specific time in the past.
"I visited my grandmother yesterday."
Signal words: yesterday, ago, last week, in 2021.
2. Present Perfect (Connected to Now)
Use for life experiences, recent results, or unfinished states.
"I have lost my keys (I can't get in now)."
Signal words: ever, never, just, already, yet, since, for.
3. Past Perfect (The Earlier Past)
Use in storytelling when one past event happened BEFORE another past event.
"When I got to class, the lesson had started."
Signal frames: by the time, when, after, realised that.

Meaning & Form

The main question to ask yourself is: Is the time finished, or is the action connected to NOW?

🌟 The 3 Core B1 Uses of Present Perfect

1. Life Experience
Any time in your life up to now. Exact time is not important.
"Have you ever seen a shooting star?"
"I've never been to Japan."
2. Recent Action with a Present Result
Something happened recently and affects right now.
"I've lost my keys." (Result: I can't open the door now).
"She has just finished her exam!"
3. Unfinished Time or State
Started in the past and continues right now.
"I've lived here for three years." (I still live here).
"I've had two coffees today." (Today is not over).

🔍 Minimal Contrast Pairs (Pictures in Words)

"I lost my keys yesterday."
Specific past time. Maybe I found them later or got a spare key.
"I've lost my keys!"
Happened recently. I still don't have them right now — please help me look!
"She lived in London for 2 years."
Finished. She lives in Istanbul or Madrid now.
"She's lived in London for 2 years."
Unfinished. She still lives in London today.

🇹🇷 Turkish Learner L1 Focus Note (Neden Karıştırıyoruz?)

Neden zorlanırız? Türkçe'de "Present Perfect" zamanının birebir karşılığı yoktur. Hem "Dün sinemaya gittim" (Past Simple) hem de "Daha önce hiç suşi yedim mi?" (Present Perfect) cümlelerinde Türkçe'de -di'li geçmiş zaman kullanırız.

3 Altın Kural:

  • Cümlede net bir geçmiş zaman zarfı (yesterday, 2 days ago, in 2020, when I was...) varsa kesinlikle Past Simple (V2) kullanılır.
  • Hayat tecrübesi soruyorsanız (Have you ever...?), zaman önemli değildir; Present Perfect kullanılır. Cevap verirken detay ve zaman verirseniz Past Simple'a geçiş yapın ("Yes, I have. I went there last summer.").
  • "-den beri" (since / for) diyorsanız ve eylem hâlâ sürüyorsa Türkçe'de geniş zaman kullansak bile İngilizce'de Present Perfect şarttır: "I live here for 2 years" ❌ -> "I have lived here for 2 years" ✅.
❓ Concept Checking Questions (CCQs) for Teachers
  1. "I have lost my passport." -> Do I have my passport in my hand now? (No.) Is the time finished or is the problem right now? (Right now.)
  2. "Did you go to London in 2022?" -> Are we in 2022 now? (No.) Is the time finished? (Yes.)
  3. "He's lived here since May." -> Did he start in May? (Yes.) Does he live here today? (Yes.)
  4. "I saw that movie yesterday." -> Do we know the exact day? (Yes, yesterday.) Can we use 'have seen'? (No, because yesterday is a finished time.)
📙 Module B: Past Simple vs Past Perfect
Storytelling Tense

We use the Past Perfect (had + past participle) for ONE main purpose at B1: Storytelling (The Earlier Past).

📖 Visual Timeline: Two Past Events (Which Happened First?)
Event 1 (First)
PAST PERFECT (had left)
Event 2 (Second)
PAST SIMPLE (arrived)
NOW
Present conversation

"When we arrived at the station, the train had already left." (Event 1: Train left -> Event 2: We arrived -> Result: We missed the train!).

🗣️ Spoken Form Focus: The Contraction "I'd" /aɪd/

In natural spoken English, native speakers almost always contract had:

• "I had forgotten my wallet." -> "I'd forgotten my wallet." (/aɪd fərˈɡɒt.ən/)
• "She had already left." -> "She'd already left." (/ʃiːd ɔːlˈred.i left/)

🎬 5 High-Frequency Storytelling Frames

1. "By the time I arrived..."
"By the time I arrived at the party, everyone had gone home."
2. "I realised that I had..."
"When I got to the airport, I realised I had left my passport at home."
3. "When + Past Simple, + Past Perfect"
"When the film started, we had already eaten all our popcorn."
4. "After + Past Perfect, + Past Simple"
"After he had finished his homework, he went out with his friends."
💡 B1 Golden Rule: Keep It Simple!
You only need Past Perfect when the time order is confusing or when you want to highlight which action happened first. If you use "before" or "after", the order is already clear, so Past Simple is also very natural in speaking:
"I had dinner before I went out." (Perfect and natural!).

🔍 Meaning Contrast: Did you catch the film?

"When we arrived, the film started."
First we arrived, and then immediately the film started. We watched the whole movie!
"When we arrived, the film had started."
The film started BEFORE we arrived. We were late and missed the opening scene!
❓ Concept Checking Questions (CCQs) for Teachers
  1. "When Sarah called, John had left." -> Who left first, John or Sarah? (John.) Did Sarah speak to John? (No, he was already gone.)
  2. "By the time the bus came, I had waited for 40 minutes." -> Which action was longer? (Waiting.) Did the waiting start before the bus arrived? (Yes.)
  3. "I ate lunch after I did my homework." -> Do we understand which happened first? (Yes, 'after' makes it clear.) Is Past Simple fine here? (Yes, completely natural!).
